Item Tracking

An offline inventory manager for home, pantry, or small-business stock. Built in SwiftUI, persisted with SwiftData, and shipped to the App Store.

Problem

People lose track of what they have and where it lives — pantry, garage, office. Spreadsheets are clumsy on a phone. Item Tracking keeps a simple location → item → quantity model, with a ledger so you can see what went in or out and when.

Architecture

The app is offline-first. There is no remote backend. SwiftData stores three models: InventoryLocation, LocationItem, and a local User. Quantity changes are recorded as Transaction objects (add/remove, optional expiration, supplier, custom fields). A NotificationManager polls stock and expiration against global or per-item thresholds.

Flow. Locations list → item grid → Items In / History / Items Out. Removals can target a specific purchase bundle so expiration dates stay attached to the right batch.

What I built

Locations & items

Create, rename, delete, and reorder locations. Each location has a two-column item grid with live quantities and quick-add suggestions.

Transaction ledger

Every in/out is a dated transaction. History is per item. Custom field templates persist purchase details across later adds.

Alerts

Low-stock and expiration notifications. Global defaults or item-specific thresholds. Immediate, next-day 9 AM, or weekly cadence.

Export & account

CSV export for selected locations via the system share sheet. Local sign-in, profile photo, appearance, privacy and terms.

Why this design

Keeping data on device made privacy simple (Apple lists “Data Not Collected”) and let the app work without an account. The ledger is more work than incrementing a number, but it is what makes expiration and “which batch did this come from” honest. Shipping to the App Store forced the usual production details: entitlements, notifications, and a versioned release (currently 1.2.1).
